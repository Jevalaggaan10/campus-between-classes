    How the timing vectors work

    Each course has two sparse 965-dimensional vectors: five weekdays with 193 five-minute bins, one channel for starts and one for endings. Expected weekly frequencies preserve dated and fortnightly meetings.

    Arrival pressure spans 35 minutes before to 10 minutes after a start, peaking 10 minutes before. Departure pressure spans five minutes before to 40 minutes after an ending, peaking 10 minutes afterward. These are planning assumptions.

    Two academic-quarter scenarios preserve explicit corrections. The maps average them; the sorter uses the higher score, rounded to 0.1 for ties. The range expresses timing sensitivity, not statistical confidence. There are 683 retained patterns with uncertain time convention.

    Coverage, route traps and limitations

    The catalogs are overlapping public sources, not a complete census. The default model excludes Homburg and unverified campus locations. Course size, actual attendance, route shares, individual final classes and other passengers remain unknown.

    Some morning 109 trips at Campus :29 terminate at Rathaus. Many 112s terminate at Hauptbahnhof. The 101 return table omits the HTW/SWS minute, so it was not invented. Only verified direct home journeys enter the ranking.

    There are 165 distinct HTW/SWS timetable options; 160 fit the model’s 06:00 to 22:00 hours, producing 800 weekday-expanded options. Exact clock times come from operator PDFs valid from 1 July 2026, checked 26 October. Check the journey planner for date-specific changes.
