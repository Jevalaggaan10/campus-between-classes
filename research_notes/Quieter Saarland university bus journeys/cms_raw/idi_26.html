    Ideen der Informatik

    Organisational:

    - Successful completion of the course will give you 5 ECTS. The course consists of a weekly 90-min lecture, a weekly voluntary assignment and a final written exam. We are open for all students not enrolled in a study program at the Computer Science department.
    - It takes place on Fridays, 8-10am c.t., in E1.7 R.301, starting on October 16th, 2026, ending February 5th, 2027. We encourage in-person participation, however, we will both stream and record the lectures, if you cannot attend physically.
    - Here the Microsoft Teams link for the lecture.
    - Recordings and all other content will be made available via CMS.
    - Course language is English.
    - Lectures consists of two parts: First 30-min discussion of last week's assignment. The remaining 60-min new input. A week's assignment builds on previous weeks input.
    - The assignments are voluntary, thus not graded. We offer students to provide individual feedback on their assignments. We expect students who use this offer not to hand in LLM-generated answers.
    - The final exam is in-person, date tbd. However, we also offer remote exams on request. More on remote exams below.

    Contents and goals:

    Three goals:

    • Familiarize the audience with basic concepts in Computer Science: What is an algorithm? What is a computer? What is data?
    • Understand the interplay between information systems and society: How does technology transform society? How can technology be used to measure societal changes?
    • Gain enough knowledge to critically discuss various impacts of information systems on the society.

    We address following topics: history of computer science, algorithms, programming languages, technical concepts of data centres, search engines, large language models, cryptography, networks, block chains, security and privacy and societal impacts. A detailed curriculum will be added soon.

    We do not expect any prior knowledge about these topics. However, we highly welcome critical thinking and active involvement in the discussions.
