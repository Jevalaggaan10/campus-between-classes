Introduction to Algorithms and Data Structures
This lecture gives an introduction to the design of efficient algorithms and data structures, as well as analyzing their correctness and running time. We will discuss algorithm design techniques such as divide and conquer, exhaustive search, greedy and dynamic programming as well as randomized algorithms and amortized analysis of algorithms.
Lecture Format
Lecture: Tuesday 12:15-13:45 (Location: GHH E2.2)
The first lecture will take place on October 13, 2026 . The lectures will be held in English.
Prerequisites: It is recommended to have taken Programming 1+2 and Mathematics for Computer Scientists 1+2 before this course.
Exercise Sheets & Tutorials
This course has the goal that participants become familiar with fundamental concepts of algorithms, data structures, and their analysis. Students should become able to recognize algorithmic or storage situations and to apply appropriate methods and justify the choice of these methods. Such a familiarity and facility with the subject matter can hardly be achieved just by consuming various materials, be it attending lectures, viewing videos or slide presentations, or reading somebody else's write-ups. Instead you need to study the ideas, mull them over, grapple with the details, and possibly discuss your ideas with others. And, of course, solving exercise problems will be extremely helpful.
However, solving exercise problems will only be useful if it happens by your own efforts. Copying somebody else's solutions or appealing to ChatGPT or the like will not really help you much with gaining familiarity with the subject matter. Of course easy availability of such solution sources makes it all the more tempting to use them.
In order to minimize this temptation we have removed all grade relevance from the exercise sets. We will offer such a set each week. You are free to work on them or ignore them. If you turn in a solution we will give you feedback (some of which may be in form of points). However,your handed in solutions will not directly influence your grade or play a role in the qualification for the final exams (everybody registered for the course qualifies). In such a setting it really makes no sense to  turn in solutions that are not your own. You would just be wasting  resources, in particular your time, and, more importantly the time of  the tutors. Their time would be much better spent answering your questions.
Exercise sheets will be handed out on Wednesday Morning at 8:00 AM and are due on the next Tuesday 11:59AM before the lecture. Why the deadline? For one, it seems to be human nature that such a pacing helps with getting things done.  But more importantly, tutors need to be able to manage their time. You can turn in solutions late, but in this case we do not guarantee to look at them and provide feedback.
You are free to work in groups.  But in this case please do not turn in multiple duplicate solutions. You should be able to share the feedback.
