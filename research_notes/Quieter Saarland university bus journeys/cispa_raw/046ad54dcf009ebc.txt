Content
This course explores usable security and privacy, a field that examines how people interact with security & privacy technologies, and how those technologies can be designed to actually work for them. Across the semester we will read and discuss papers from the last decade (2016-2026) covering subfields, such as security advice, authentication, scams & fraud, privacy & consent, at-risk users, and emerging threats to name a few. Students will learn to read papers critically, connect findings across the broader field, and present research effectively to their peers.

Class Structure
The course runs as weekly, module-based sessions. Each module corresponds to one subfield within usable security and privacy.
ATTENDANCE IS MANDATORY. If you cannot attend on these dates, please don't register for the seminar.
Critiques
Each week's module comes with 3 readings: 2 compulsory and 1 optional. Every week, students will be required to submit a critique on
one
of the compulsory readings for that week's module. The critique for the week will be due on
Mondays 3 PM
before every session. Since each session happens on a
Tuesday
, and the critique is due the
Monday before
at
3 PM
, that means the deadline falls on the day
before
the week's session.
All students submit a critique on one of the compulsory readings via CMS AND publicly in a discussion forum before the presentation day.
Instructions on how to write a critique will be provided in the first few sessions and will be made available in the Materials tab.
Presentations
Every module will be a student-led discussion and presentation of that week's
compulsory
readings. One student presents the
compulsory
readings for that module. As pointed out in the section above, each week's module comes with 3 readings: 2 compulsory and 1 optional. Rather than presenting each paper separately, the presenter is expected to synthesize
the compulsory readings
of that week's readings into a single, cohesive talk. The presentation should draw connections across the papers, weaving them into one narrative about the module's topic rather than summarizing them one by one.
The presenter may incorporate classmates' critiques into their presentation, if they choose to. Students are encouraged, but not required, to conduct additional research on current affairs and additional papers to strengthen their talk as they see fit.
Students will be judged on content, clarity, interactiveness, ability to tackle questions, and engagement with external materials.
More details on how to present the module, including an example presentation, will be provided in the sessions. A grading rubric will also be made available in the Materials tab.

Academic Dishonesty
In this course, you will be reading a wide range of scholarly sources and will work to integrate these concepts into your own research, critiques and professional practice. It is imperative that any use of others' intellectual work (including, but not limited to, quotations, paraphrasing of ideas, figures, models) be accompanied by an appropriate citation or reference. The course follows Saarland University and DFG's guidelines regarding academic dishonesty: https://www.uni-saarland.de/en/administration/research-integrity.html
Any form of cheating will result in a penalty up to automatic fail grade for the course. All instances of cheating will be forwarded to the associated university body for appropriate disciplinary action.
Helping another student to cheat and cheating are considered equal cases of academic dishonesty and will be treated as outlined above.
Giving another student an electronic copy of your homework, or access to your computer account constitutes cheating on your behalf if that other student copies or uses any files that become implicated in a cheating case.
AI Policy
The use of AI tools (eg, ChatGPT, Claude, or similar) is
strictly prohibited
for all coursework in this seminar, including critiques and presentations. All critiques and presentation content must be the student's own original work and analysis. Any case of plagiarism will be reported directly to the Examination Office.
