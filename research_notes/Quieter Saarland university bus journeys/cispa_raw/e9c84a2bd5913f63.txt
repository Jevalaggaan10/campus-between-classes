Content
In this seminar, we will
learn
how cybersecurity is practically implemented in organizations (enterprises, public agencies, NGOs, etc.) and discuss why academic cybersecurity concepts are often not followed in practice. We will
read
and discuss papers on cybersecurity in organizations, frequently utilizing human-centered research methods such as interviews, surveys, focus groups, and case studies.
We will
read
various recent scientific publications on cybersecurity in organizational practice and foundational papers, some of which have been around for over two decades. We will
discuss
those papers in the seminar based on
presentations
by student teams. Throughout the seminar, we will
reflect
upon our own (current and future) experience with cybersecurity in organizational practice. We will
learn
to negotiate cybersecurity strategies with other stakeholders in our future professions.
Topics might include
Organizational Security Research Methods,
Regulations, Norms, and Frameworks,
Various Roles in Organizational Security,
Human-Centered Security,
Economics of Information Security,
Organizational (Security) Leadership, Change Management, Communication,
Secure Software Development in Organizations, and
How to Find and Read Scientific Papers.

Registration & Prerequisites
All students must register using the
central seminar assignment system.
There are no formal prerequisites for this course. However, to succeed in this seminar, you should consider the following:
You should be interested in perfecting your presentation skills. Throughout the seminar, you will give multiple (smaller) presentations, and we expect you to progress in your skills over time.
You should be interested in learning to read and understand a variety of research papers, their methods, and their limitations, and you should be willing to discuss this.
You should be interested in questioning your position and approach to cybersecurity and able to consider the perspectives of various organizational stakeholders.
You must be interested in interdisciplinary perspectives on cybersecurity, expanding the mathematical and engineering approach to a more human-centered perspective.
